## Formula

    curvature relation:
      (k₁ + k₂ + k₃ + k₄)² = 2(k₁² + k₂² + k₃² + k₄²)
      k₄ = k₁ + k₂ + k₃ ± 2√(k₁k₂ + k₂k₃ + k₃k₁)

    complex-coordinate form (centres z, curvatures k):
      k₄·z₄ = k₁z₁ + k₂z₂ + k₃z₃ ± 2√(k₁k₂·z₁z₂ + k₂k₃·z₂z₃ + k₃k₁·z₃z₁)

## What it means

Descartes' theorem says that whenever four circles are mutually tangent — each one touching the other three — their curvatures (k = 1/radius, with an *internally* tangent enclosing circle counted as negative) satisfy that fixed quadratic relation. Given any three mutually tangent circles, the relation is a quadratic in the fourth curvature, and it always has two solutions: one is the circle already known if you started from a quadruple, the other (the ± root) is the *other* circle tangent to all three — geometrically, the small gap left in the middle of any three touching circles, or the much larger circle that could enclose all three from outside. This pattern uses exactly that second-root trick to grow the whole packing: from a starting triple it computes the missing fourth circle, then treats that circle as a new member of three fresh triples and recurses.

The complex form is what makes this practical without any trigonometry or circle-intersection geometry at all: representing each circle's centre as a complex number z lets the same ± quadratic solve for both curvature *and* position simultaneously, because multiplying and adding complex numbers naturally encodes the rotations and translations that would otherwise need to be worked out case by case. That's the "complex-coordinate form" — centres carried as z rather than (x, y) pairs — and it's exactly what the pattern's descartesRoots step computes: the same weighted sum and cross-term square root as the curvature formula, just carried through in ℂ instead of ℝ.

Starting from an outer bounding circle and two equal circles tangent to each other and to it, the fourth (asymmetric) seed circle isn't picked by hand — it's generated by applying Descartes' theorem to that first triple, so the whole packing, seed included, comes from one recursive rule applied four different ways to the four faces of the starting tetrad of mutually tangent circles.

## Parameters

- **maxDepth** — how many generations of the recursion are allowed; each generation fills the gaps left by the previous one with smaller circles, so higher values push the packing toward its infinite, fractal limit.
- **minRadius** — the radius, in pixels, below which a computed circle is discarded instead of drawn or recursed into further; this is what actually terminates the (otherwise infinite) recursion in practice.
- **strokeWidth** — the line weight used to draw each circle; a drawing choice, not part of Descartes' theorem.
- **fillAlternate** — shades circles from even recursion depths with a light fill instead of leaving them empty; a drawing choice used to make the generational structure visible at a glance.
