## Formula

    Ellipse: (x/A)² + (y/B)² = 1,   B = A·√(1−e²)
    From a boundary point, follow the ray until it hits the boundary again:
    the exit distance solves a quadratic whose other root is 0, so s = −2·(P·d)ₑ / (d·d)ₑ
    Reflect specularly: d′ = d − 2(d·n̂)n̂, with n̂ ∝ (x/A², y/B²)

## What it means

Roll a billiard ball — or shine a light ray — inside an elliptical table and let it bounce a few hundred times, each bounce a perfect mirror reflection. The chords the ray traces are all straight lines, but they refuse to fill the table evenly: they crowd along a boundary the ray never crosses, and that boundary is a smooth curve nobody drew.

That curve is the billiard's *caustic*, and its existence is a theorem, not an accident. Elliptical billiards are integrable: besides its energy, the bouncing ray conserves a second quantity (the product of its angular momenta about the two foci), and that conservation pins every chord of a single orbit tangent to one fixed conic *confocal* with the table — sharing its two foci. If the ray passes outside the foci, the caustic is a smaller ellipse and the chords weave an annulus. If it passes between them, the caustic is a hyperbola and the chords gather into an hourglass. Launch a ray exactly through one focus and it must pass through the other forever after — the reflector property of the ellipse, the reason whispering galleries work.

This is the same magic as the times-table chords: hundreds of straight lines conspiring to conjure a curve that exists only as their envelope — but here the conspiracy is enforced by a conservation law rather than by modular arithmetic.

## Parameters

**Eccentricity** shapes the table from near-circle to slender oval, dragging the foci apart. **Launch** is the angle between the first chord and the wall: shallow angles hug the rim and give elliptic caustics, steep ones cross between the foci and give hyperbolic ones. **Bounces** sets how many chords build the web. The rim outline can be hidden to leave the caustic floating alone.
